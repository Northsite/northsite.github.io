The shortlist got shorter

A search results page gives ten links and lets the customer choose. An AI answer gives two or three names and a reason. If you are not one of them, you were not beaten on price or on service — you were left out of the sentence before the customer knew you existed.

01

It reads facts, not marketing

Assistants weigh things they can verify from more than one place: your name, address, phone, hours, category, services, and what customers actually wrote in reviews. Adjectives on your homepage count for nothing.

02

Inconsistency is the killer

An old suite number on one directory and a new one on another makes a model less sure you are real. Less sure means left out. Most local businesses lose here without ever knowing it.

03

Answers beat brochures

Pages that answer a direct question — "do you charge for emergency callouts?" — get quoted. Pages that describe how passionate your team is do not. That is a writing problem, and it is fixable this week.

The 30-minute action plan, free

This is the book's appendix, reproduced in full. If you only do this much and never buy anything, you will still be ahead of most of your competitors. Do it in this order.

0–5 min

Baseline test

Open ChatGPT and ask two things: "Who are the best [your trade] companies in [your city]?" and "Is [Your Business Name] a real, currently operating business in [your city]?" Write down exactly what comes back, including who else gets named. This is your before picture, and you will want it in a month.

5–15 min

Google Business Profile quick pass

Log into business.google.com. Confirm name, address, phone, hours and primary category are correct and current. Add three real services to your Services list if any are missing. Upload two recent photos. This single listing does more work than anything else on this page.

15–20 min

NAP spot-check

Compare the phone number and address in your website footer against your Google listing, character for character. Suite numbers, "St" versus "Street", an old number still ringing somewhere. Fix any mismatch immediately.

20–25 min

Send one review request

Pick your most recent genuinely satisfied customer and ask, today, with your Google review link. Ask them to mention the specific job and the city — a review that says "replaced our water heater in Etobicoke" is worth several that say "great service".

25–30 min

Pick your next move

Choose one thing for this week: a dedicated page for your highest-demand service, an FAQ section answering what customers actually ask, or LocalBusiness schema on your homepage. Put it in the calendar on a specific day. Not someday.

Questions people ask before buying

Do I need to be technical to use this?

No. Every chapter is written for the person who runs the business, not a developer. The one technical chapter, on schema markup, hands you code you can copy and paste or forward to whoever built your site, with plain-English notes on what each line is doing and why.

Is this just SEO with a new name?

There is overlap, because AI assistants read the same web that search engines index. The difference is what wins. Classic SEO fights for a blue link on page one. An AI answer names two or three businesses and stops, and it favours the ones whose facts are consistent everywhere and whose pages answer questions directly. That changes what you work on first, and what you can safely ignore.

How long before it makes a difference?

Google Business Profile corrections can surface within days. Review signals and new service pages take weeks. Nothing here is an overnight switch — which is exactly why it is worth doing. The businesses getting named in AI answers today are the ones that fixed the boring details months ago.

Does it apply outside the US and Canada?

Yes. The examples are North American, but Google Business Profile, review platforms, schema markup, and the way language models weigh consistent public information work the same way anywhere those services operate.
